## 2. Creative technology survey

_Produced by a parallel agent against the same codebase; verdicts and effort estimates are its own._

# zadum — technology survey: better spec, fewer questions

Grounded in: `selector.ts` (3 VOI scorings, 2-ply lookahead, θ replay-calibrated), `worlds.ts` (K=12 point-assignment particles, ε-soft conditioning, ESS-triggered rejuvenation, α prior mixing), `catalog.ts` (~49–65 nodes/session, 2–5 options, ~10–15 hard edges per catalog), `harness/run.ts` (sweep, θ curve, judge), `learning/` (population priors, ECE, θ replay, phrasing bandit — built, unwired), mining stages 1–3. Nothing below re-proposes those.

## (a) Summary

The selector is already the right shape — analytic VOI over a sampled belief, calibrated stopping, harness-gated changes — and the live evidence (all four scoring arms within ~1pp AUC) says the bottleneck is **not** question-ordering sophistication. The real leverage is elsewhere: (1) the belief representation — 12 point-assignment particles is the crudest possible approximation of a small discrete posterior, and it is the proven root cause of the live failure modes (ESS collapse, implication cascades, joint_entropy's log₂K ceiling); a Rao-Blackwellized persona-mixture belief fixes the disease ADR-020 treats symptomatically. (2) The objective's weights — `consequence` is hand-set 0–5, and every scoring criterion multiplies by it; measured sensitivity weights would improve all arms at once. (3) The stopping UX — the founder's information-gain curve is cheap (the data is already in `card_shown` events), directly addresses "no hardcoded question count", and converts θ from a silent guillotine into a visible, overridable recommendation. Meanwhile a neat structural fact makes the "reconstruct the whole tree" instinct partially right: the *optimal policy over the particle belief* is exactly computable by DP over particle subsets (≤2^K states), which settles greedy-vs-lookahead empirically and forever — as a harness diagnostic, not a runtime component.

## (c) Full survey with verdicts

### 1. The information-gain curve (founder's idea) — **worth it, do it soon**

The data already exists: every `card_shown` logs `value1`, `share`, and settledness is computable per step. Design points:

- **Statistic to plot: `share`, not `value1`.** `value1`'s units differ per scoring and drift with catalog size; `share` (value1 ÷ total remaining uncertainty, already computed as the "scale-free diagnostic") is 0–1 and comparable across archetypes. Big number: **settledness** ("your spec is 87% settled"), which is monotone-ish and non-technical. Per-card bars: marginal share. This is exactly the loss-curve intuition the founder wants, in units a bookkeeper understands.
- **θ complements, the curve doesn't replace it.** θ is the calibrated price of a tap; the curve is why-you-should-believe-it. Recommended UX: when `decideNext` returns `converged`, do not silently stop — render "the next question would settle less than ~2% of what's left — stop here?" with *stop* as the default action and *keep going* live. When the user keeps going, keep dealing by rank with no θ (they've re-priced their own tap at ~0); stop only at `no_open`. This resolves the founder's (a)/(b)/(c) trilemma with "recommend + default-accept": auto behavior for the passive user, no hard limit for the engaged one.
- **The 12-card cap** should become a cap on *unsolicited* cards only (Rule 7 rephrased: "never *demand* more than 12"), which honors "qty must not be hardcoded" without opening the door to a 40-question interrogation by default. One-line change in `decideNext` call sites + a product-rule edit.
- One caution: don't plot *recovery* (unknowable at runtime) and don't let the curve imply precision the 12-particle belief doesn't have — show share as coarse bars, not a smooth line.

### 2. Reconstructing the entire tree — **the full tree: no; the particle-DP: yes, as a diagnostic; opening books: not yet**

Honest quantification. Assignment space: ~3^50 ≈ 10^23 — no. Belief-MDP over exact beliefs: histories ≈ (50·3)^12 ≈ 10^26 — no. Order-invariance (conditioning commutes) collapses histories to answer-*sets*: C(50,12)·3^12 ≈ 10^18 — still no. **But**: the engine's belief is not exact — it is K=12 particles, and hard conditioning can only *zero subsets of particles*. So the reachable belief space per session is bounded by subsets of the initial particle set: ≤2^12 = 4096 weight-patterns (fewer in practice). An exact finite-horizon DP (value iteration over surviving-subset states, reward = recovery proxy or entropy removed, action = which node to ask) is **a few hundred lines and milliseconds**. What it buys: the *optimal* policy against the particle approximation — an upper bound no greedy/2-ply/MCTS variant can beat. If greedy ≈ DP-optimal (my prediction, given the 1pp sweep spread), the entire "should we search deeper" question is closed with a proof-shaped artifact instead of recurring sweeps. If not, you learn exactly where lookahead pays. Harness-only; the runtime never changes. Caveat to state honestly in the report it generates: optimality is w.r.t. the particles, and real sessions use soft conditioning + resampling, so treat it as a bound, not a shipping policy.

**Opening books / offline MCTS per archetype**: weak fit today because worlds are sampled *conditioned on the drafted Sheet*, so the belief at card 1 is project-specific, not archetype-generic; a book computed on archetype priors would be stale by the time it's consulted, and speculative precompute already fills the latency gap. Revisit only if the DP diagnostic shows greedy losing meaningfully at depth ≥3 (unlikely). **POMCP/SARSOP**: superseded by the subset-DP at this scale — those solvers earn their complexity at state spaces the DP can't enumerate.

### 3. ML on the tree / session logs — **not for selection; yes (later) for priors and weights**

- **Next-question policy (xgboost/DNN, policy distillation)**: don't. The analytic selector runs in milliseconds (no latency to amortize), is within noise of every alternative tried (no accuracy gap demonstrated), and is auditable line-by-line — a policy model surrenders explainability and the "harness decides" discipline for nothing measurable. Distillation makes sense when the teacher is *slow* (real MCTS, big rollouts); here the teacher is already fast.
- **Learned answer priors** — yes, this is where ML genuinely belongs, and the scaffolding exists (`population_priors.ts`, deliberately unwired). The v2 of that is an *embedding-conditioned* prior: P(option | one-liner embedding, archetype, node) via multinomial logistic regression or small GBM, replacing the flat per-archetype counts once there are ~300+ sessions (rule of thumb: ≥20 observations per (node, option) cell after shrinkage — below that, hierarchical shrinkage already built is strictly better). Wire `mixWithCatalog` behind a harness gate first; the fancy version needs real users, not more code.
- **Learned stopping**: covered better by θ replay (`theta_replay.ts`) — a survival model adds nothing until post-session edit data (Loop-C signal #1) exists.
- **Consequence weights**: the *right* target for offline computation, but by sensitivity analysis (flip → recompile → diff), not supervised ML — see top-5 #3. LEARNING.md already sketches it; my contribution is priority: it dominates any selector work because every scoring arm multiplies by these hand-set numbers. When post-session churn data arrives, blend measured-sensitivity with observed-churn exactly as the doc proposes.

### 4. Search techniques — **greedy is probably already near-optimal; add the guarantee-bearing objective, skip runtime search**

- **Adaptive submodularity (the honest math)**: greedy's celebrated (1−1/e)-approximation (Golovin & Krause 2011) requires the objective to be adaptive submodular and adaptive monotone. **Shannon information gain is *not* adaptive submodular in general** — neither is Σcₙ·H(Pₙ) reduction — so the current greedy has no worst-case guarantee. Version-space reduction (generalized binary search) *is* adaptive submodular, but it's consequence-blind. The fix from the active-learning literature is **EC² / Decision Region Determination** (Golovin–Krause–Ray 2010; Javdani et al. 2014): put worlds in equivalence classes by *what spec they compile to* (≈ their assignments restricted to high-consequence nodes), weight "edges" between worlds in different classes, value a question by expected weight of edges cut. EC² is adaptive submodular ⇒ greedy is provably within (1 + ln(1/η)) of optimal expected cost. Philosophically it is exactly this product's objective: you don't need to *know* everything, you need to *not ship the wrong spec*. Implementation: a 4th `Scoring` over the same particles, ~150 lines, one sweep arm. The harness decides, per house rules.
- **Runtime MCTS / beam search over question sequences**: don't — see §2; the subset-DP bounds what any of them could gain, at zero runtime risk.
- **Binary search analogy**: already embodied (joint_entropy *is* "halve the possibility space"), and the live data already shows its weakness (log₂K ceiling with K=12 — another argument for top-5 #1, which raises the effective support far beyond 12 point masses).
- **Batch / non-adaptive design**: the multi-option card (ADR-026) already captures most of the batch win per interaction step; true batched VOI (submodular set selection of several cards shown at once) fights the one-card-at-a-time UX. Not worth it.

### 5. Neuro-symbolic — **the most interesting question; the answer is a hybrid, not a swap**

- **Would exact inference beat 12 particles?** The catalog alone (priors + hard edges) is *not* where the value is: strip the LLM sampler and the model factorizes into near-independent nodes plus a sparse implication DAG — exact inference is trivial and *worthless*, because everything the belief knows beyond the catalog prior comes from the sampler's persona-level correlations ("solo bookkeeper" worlds co-vary tenancy, roles, billing). Those correlations live only in the samples. So: pure symbolic — no.
- **The right hybrid — Rao-Blackwellized mixture (top-5 #1)**: keep the persona as the particle, make everything below it analytic. The sampler emits, per persona, a *distribution* per node (schema change: `assignment: nodeId → optionId` becomes `nodeId → {optionId: weight}` — still ADR-011-conservative, flat records). Belief = mixture of products: P(n=a) = Σ_p w_p·P_p(n=a). An answer updates persona weights by likelihood P_p(answer) — never zeroing support — and VOI has closed forms under the mixture. Consequences: ESS collapse and the ADR-020 implication-cascade class disappear structurally (no point mass to deplete); joint_entropy's ceiling lifts; `conditionHard`/`conditionSoft`/`distribution` change, `selector.ts` barely does. The current M-persona design is already query-by-committee; this is the committee done properly. Risk: LLM-verbalized per-node probabilities are poorly calibrated — mitigate by quantizing to {0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 0.9}-style buckets in the schema and letting α-mixing smooth the rest; the harness arbitrates as always.
- **Learned structure (Chow–Liu tree / low-treewidth model over accumulated worlds per archetype)**: a principled way to turn thousands of sampled worlds into a *correlated prior* (replacing independent per-node priors) with exact junction-tree inference at ~49 nodes. Worth it later, when per-archetype samples accumulate across sessions; strictly after #1, which it composes with (mixture over personas × tree-structured conditionals).
- **SAT/CP for world repair**: current `repairAssignment` (fixpoint propagation + prior-argmax fill; 26 repairs/6 conflicts per 12 worlds live) is adequate for ~15 edges. A weighted-MaxSAT "minimal prior-weighted repair" is the right upgrade *only if* catalogs grow rich edge structure (50+ edges, cycles). Cheaper first step, already half-noted in STATUS: feed the observed conflicts back into the sampler prompt. Not now.

### 6. Other AI capabilities

- **Embeddings for the recall metric** — worth it now. Bipartite matching over cosine similarity (any small local embedding model, or provider embeddings cached to disk) as `embedding_recall` beside the LLM judge: deterministic, ~free, runs in CI on every commit where the judge is too slow/expensive. Judge stays the gold-standard arbiter; embeddings catch regressions daily. `harness/judge.ts` sibling, ~2 days. (Offline only — no runtime network dependency, so Rule 8 intact.)
- **Embeddings for exemplar retrieval** (similar past Sheets as drafter few-shots) — already in LEARNING.md's ladder; agreed, at n≈100.
- **Conformal prediction for defaults** — worth it later, lovely fit. Split-conformal over (confidence, was-overridden) pairs gives a distribution-free guarantee: "defaults shown at ≥q confidence are correct ≥90% of the time" — exactly the honest sentence the defaults-review UI wants to print. Pure arithmetic on events (`calibration.ts` already collects the pairs); needs ~100+ labeled defaults to be non-vacuous. A perfect Loop-B citizen.
- **Query-by-committee** — already implicitly built (M personas = committee; vote disagreement = the entropy the selector reads). The formal upgrade is top-5 #1.
- **GFlowNets / DPP-diverse world sampling** — GFlowNets: no (research machinery for a 12-particle problem). The cheap 80%: sample a larger candidate pool (batches already exist) and select K by a determinantal/greedy-diversity criterion so particles cover modes instead of duplicating the archetype's central persona. Low effort, harness-gated, and it directly raises effective support — but #1 obviates most of it.
- **Collaborative-filtering priors (users × decisions matrix factorization)** — v3 at n≈10⁴ sessions; hierarchical shrinkage (built) is the correct small-n version of the same idea. Don't build early.
- **Question synthesis beyond the catalog** — the planner's bespoke nodes + Loop C promotion already own this. One addition worth making when Loop C lands: mine the "something else" free-texts specifically — each is a user telling you the catalog's option set is wrong, the highest-signal catalog-evolution event there is.

### 7. Anything else

- **Sensitivity-measured consequence weights** (top-5 #3) — restated because it's the quiet keystone: ADR-014's defense of weighted_entropy over joint_entropy *is* the consequence weights; today they're vibes on a 0–5 scale. One offline run per catalog version: for each node, flip the reference world's option, recompile (mock-compile or cheap-tier), measure normalized spec diff (section-weighted token/AST distance), normalize to the 0–5 scale, ship as catalog data with the version tag. Also fixes `secondaryArchetypeWeight`'s 0.5 from "plausible constant" to measured.
- **Sim-to-real answer-noise model**: the harness's simulated user answers perfectly from gold; real users mis-tap, satisfice, and misread. Injecting a calibrated error rate (even a flat 5–10% wrong-option rate) into `sim_user` would stress-test ε and the implication guards before real users do. Trivial effort, prevents an entire class of live surprise.
- **Provenance-weighted belief updates**: answers arriving via `user_edit` prose (patcher) are noisier than card taps; a per-source ε (edit-ε > card-ε) is a two-line generalization of `conditionSoft` the harness can price.

## (d) Don't do this

1. **Runtime MCTS / POMCP / beam search** — the subset-DP diagnostic will bound their headroom at ≈0; they add nondeterminism to a core whose determinism is the product's spine (undo, replay-calibration of θ, exact θ curves all rely on it).
2. **RL for the question policy** — no reward signal at this n; off-policy evaluation on 12-particle beliefs is noise; an unauditable policy violates the "harness decides, never argument" culture in spirit even when gated.
3. **XGBoost/DNN next-question classifier** — distills a millisecond teacher into an opaque student; solves no existing latency or accuracy problem (sweep spread: 1pp).
4. **Exact optimal decision tree over the true assignment space** — 10^23 states; also pointless, since the belief the engine actually holds is the particle set (see the tractable version, §2).
5. **Hand-authoring a full Bayes net catalog** (all pairwise dependencies as edges) — maintenance cost explodes at 49 nodes; the LLM sampler already prices correlations for free per project. Learn structure from samples (Chow–Liu, later) instead of authoring it.
6. **GFlowNets, diffusion samplers, neural spec generators** — capability cosplay at this scale; every one replaces a working analytic component with a training pipeline.
7. **Fine-tuning anything in v1** — the flywheel ladder (LEARNING.md) already places this correctly at n≈10⁴; doing it earlier burns data the estimators need.
8. **Replacing θ with the visible curve** — the curve without θ returns stopping to user willpower (the "TurboTax" promise is that the tool knows when to stop); θ without the curve stays a black box. Ship them together (§1).

## 3. "Train one model on all public specs / GitHub" — why the answer routes through mining

The founder asked whether to collapse the system into one clear thing: reconstruct the whole tree of
possibilities, scrape every public spec and repo, and train a DNN / xgboost (plus 1–3-gram TF-IDF features) into
something foundation-model-shaped for specs. The instinct is half right, and the half that is right is largely
already in the repo. Recorded here so it is not re-litigated from scratch:

- **Two different trees.** The space of *specs* is ~3⁵⁰ ≈ 10²³ per archetype — finite but not enumerable, and
  almost all of it carries ~zero probability. What you want is the *distribution* over it, which is what the
  sampled-worlds belief already is (implicitly), and what any foundation model is. The space of *question
  sequences* is ~10¹⁸ even after collapsing question order — **but** the engine's runtime belief is ≤12
  particles and questions can only zero particle subsets, so the reachable belief space is ≤2¹² = 4096 states.
  Exact DP over that is a few hundred lines and milliseconds, and yields the provably optimal policy *against
  the belief the engine actually holds*. That is the only tractable version of "reconstruct the entire tree",
  and it belongs in the harness as a bound, not in the runtime.
- **The labels aren't in the data.** The selector needs P(decision assignment | app description). GitHub
  records what was *built* — confounded by framework defaults and library choices — not what was *decided*, and
  the population (dev tools, clones, tutorials) is not the small-business CRUD apps this product serves.
  Extracting decision labels from public specs requires an LLM, at which point the pipeline *is*
  `mine:concepts` (stage 2), already run on 106 specs. Realistic yield is 10³–10⁴ noisy rows; a joint model over
  50 multi-way variables wants 10⁶⁺. Hierarchical shrinkage (built, `learning/population_priors.ts`) is the
  correct estimator at the available n.
- **TF-IDF n-grams are already used correctly** — offline, in `mining/ngrams.ts`, with PMI phraseness and a
  DF-entropy band that finds the ~50%-frequency bigrams that *are* decision axes. As runtime features they are
  strictly dominated by the LLM already in the loop, which is the foundation model over word combinations —
  and which conditions on *this user's drafted Sheet*, an input no offline model can have seen.
- **The matrix idea, fixed.** Rows should be spec-like documents (and completed sessions), not repos: behaviour
  ("invoice numbers have no gaps") is often absent from a repo or delegated to Stripe, so repo-derived cells
  would be Missing-Not-At-Random and the model would learn the extractor's blindness. Columns should be the
  catalog's ~50 behavioural decision nodes, not stack choices (auth/Postgres/pydantic) — those are exactly what
  the Design Sheet refuses to ask its user. Cells categorical with explicit `unobserved`, never a bare 0.
  Then the recommender math the founder wants (factorization, structure learning) applies directly, feeding
  priors and archetype discovery into the existing engine.
- **Testable rather than arguable** (~1 day): fill the invoicing catalog's 49 columns from 50 repos and 50 spec
  documents; compare fill-rate and inter-run extraction agreement. Prediction: repos fill ~20–30% of behavioural
  columns with low agreement, specs 2–3× that with high agreement. If that's wrong, the repo scrape earns its
  place.

**Where ML does belong** (in order of data hunger): learned answer priors via `mixWithCatalog` (built, unwired,
harness-gated) at ~10² sessions → per-node P(option | description embedding, archetype) with logistic
regression/small GBM at ~10³ → transparent joint structure learning (Chow–Liu / low-treewidth) over extracted
assignments, printable and exactly inferable at 50 nodes. Not: a learned next-question policy — the analytic
selector runs in milliseconds, sits within ~1pp of every alternative tried, and is auditable line by line.
