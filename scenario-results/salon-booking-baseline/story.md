# A Day at Bella Hair Salon — Walkthrough

1. 1. Rosa, the Salon Manager, signs in and adds a new service called Balayage, set to 2 hours and priced at $150, so it appears on the salon's service menu.
2. 2. Rosa opens stylist Maria's profile and ticks the services Maria offers — Cut & Blow-dry and Balayage — so Maria can only be booked for those two.
3. 3. Rosa sets the salon's opening hours to Tuesday–Saturday, 9:00am to 6:00pm, which becomes the window all bookings must fit inside.
4. 4. Sarah Lee, a client, signs in on her phone, picks Cut & Blow-dry ($55), chooses Maria, and books an open slot for Tuesday March 12 at 2:00pm; the booking is confirmed instantly and she gets an email and a text.
5. 5. Tom, another client, tries to book Maria for Balayage at a time Maria isn't working — that slot isn't offered, and a 45-minute cut he books frees Maria again at 2:45pm.
6. 6. A second client tries to book Maria at 2:00pm on March 12, but the slot is already taken by Sarah, so the system refuses the double-booking.
7. 7. Maria, the stylist, signs in, sees only her own appointments for the day, and blocks March 20–24 as holiday time off, making those dates unbookable for her.
8. 8. A client tries to book Maria for March 21 — the request is refused because it falls inside Maria's holiday.
9. 9. Sarah decides to cancel her March 12 appointment two days ahead; she enters the reason "schedule clash," and the salon records who cancelled, the reason, and the time before finishing the cancellation.
10. 10. Later, another client tries to cancel an appointment starting in 2 hours — inside the salon's 24-hour cut-off — so the change is blocked and she's told to phone the salon instead.
11. 11. Ben from the reception desk takes a walk-in, Tom, books him with Maria for a service she offers, and links the booking to Tom's existing client record; when the wanted slot clashes, Ben overrides it with an on-screen warning that gets recorded.
12. 12. After the visit, Maria marks Sarah's appointment as attended; a different client who never turned up is marked a no-show, which is counted against that client's record.
13. 13. When one client reaches three no-shows, her record is automatically flagged as restricted; Rosa reviews it and can lift the restriction so the client may self-book again.
14. 14. Sarah tries to open Tom's appointment from a shared link — it doesn't work, because clients can only see and change their own bookings.
15. 15. A stylist leaves, so Rosa archives their profile; every upcoming appointment is moved to another stylist who offers the same service, and those clients are notified by email and text — none are left without a stylist.
16. 16. At day's end Rosa opens the dashboard and sees today's simple totals: total bookings, no-shows, and busiest stylist; reception and stylists cannot open this screen.
